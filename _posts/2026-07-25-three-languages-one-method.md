I teach English for a living. I've stood in front of a whiteboard explaining the present perfect more times than I can count. So when people find out I'm also *learning* three languages in my spare time, the first thing they assume is that I've got some disciplined system — flashcards, a grammar workbook, a study schedule.

I don't. I have a Spotify playlist, a backlog of anime, and a stubborn habit of rewinding the same three seconds of dialogue until it clicks.

## The actual method

It's embarrassingly simple: I consume media I already love, in the language it was made in, and I let curiosity do the teaching. A song lyric I can't quite parse becomes a five-minute rabbit hole. A line of anime dialogue that doesn't match its subtitle becomes a small mystery I want to solve. None of it feels like studying, because none of it started as studying — it started as just wanting to understand something I was already enjoying.

## Why this works for me, specifically

As a teacher, I know exactly what a well-structured grammar drill looks like — and I know how little that structure has to do with why I keep coming back to my own languages. Motivation from *love of the content* turns out to be a completely different fuel than motivation from *duty to a lesson plan*. I suspect that's true for a lot of people, not just me.

## What this blog actually is

Polyglossia is where I write down what I'm picking up — Hindi through Bollywood, French through chanson, Japanese through anime. No drills, no flashcard apps. Just whatever the music or the screen happened to teach me that week, written down before I forget it.

If you're learning a language because you love the culture wrapped around it, I think you'll find something useful here too.
