I've found a format that works well enough that I want to keep repeating it: pick one album, listen all the way through, and pull out ten words or phrases I didn't know. Not fifty. Ten. Small enough to actually remember, large enough to feel like real progress.

## Why chanson specifically

Chanson française tends to prioritize wordplay and clear enunciation over speed, which makes it a gentler entry point than contemporary French pop, where slang and rapid-fire delivery can outpace a learner fast. That's not a knock on pop — it's just a different difficulty curve, and chanson has been the more forgiving one for me.

## What this particular album taught me

Beyond vocabulary, the biggest lesson was about **liaison** — the way French links word endings to the next word's beginning in ways that completely change how a sentence sounds compared to how it reads. A phrase that looks straightforward on a lyrics sheet can sound like an entirely different sequence of sounds when sung, and getting used to that gap has done more for my listening comprehension than any pronunciation guide.

## The ten words, for the record

I won't list all ten here — half the fun is that they're specific to the album, not a generic vocabulary set. But the exercise itself is the real takeaway: one album, ten words, no exceptions. It's small enough that I actually finish it every time.
